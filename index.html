04 / About

The physics of
bounded minds.

Brains and AI systems reuse the same representations across tasks. That sharing is why they generalise, and also why they can’t do several things at once or keep similar things apart. I work on that trade-off: where it comes from, how hard it binds, and whether any mind, natural or artificial, gets around it.

The same question scales up. What can a group of agents work out that none of them can alone, whether they are neurons, language models or sperm whales? The whales are not a metaphor: I’m a founding member of Project CETI, where I lead the network science.

The tools are a physicist’s: networks, information theory, statistical mechanics, and higher-order networks for when pairs won’t do. I trained in Pisa, did a PhD on complex networks at Imperial College London, and spent years in Turin at the ISI Foundation and CENTAI. Now I’m Professor of Network Science at Northeastern University London, where I run NPLab.
